# Midterm brief: Tuesday, November 3

## Bottom line
It's Election Day. Markets price Democrats at 76.5% to win the Senate and 94.5% to win the House ([Polymarket Senate](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-senate-in-2026), [House](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-house-in-2026)). The generic ballot average is D+8.6 across five aggregators as of Nov 1 ([Wikipedia aggregate table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections#Generic_congressional_ballot_aggregate_polls)). Final forecaster ratings mostly moved toward Democrats overnight.

## What moved
- **Silver Bulletin's final Senate ratings** (as of Nov 3) moved Texas, Ohio, Maine and Kansas from Tossup to Lean D, and Iowa from Tossup to Lean R ([ratings compilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_elections)).
- **Cook** moved Michigan from Tossup to Lean D. **Sabato** moved Nebraska from Lean R to Tossup (both as of Nov 2, [same source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_elections)).
- **Maine's consensus** moved back from Tossup to Lean D.
- **House:** Sabato's final ratings leave no Tossups and total 239 D to 196 R ([House ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_election_ratings)). Consensus changes: MI-10 moved from Tossup to Lean D; FL-04 and NC-14 moved from Solid R to Likely R.
- No poll average crossed a threshold.

## Races to watch today
- **Iowa (Tossup):** The average is R+0.3 across 10 polls, the closest tracked Senate race ([tracker](https://jakeworcester-byte.github.io/political-tracker/#race/SEN-IA)).
- **Kansas (Tossup, forecasters disagree):** The average is D+4.4. Ratings now run from Lean R (NBC) to Lean D (Silver Bulletin, FiftyPlusOne, Split Ticket) ([tracker](https://jakeworcester-byte.github.io/political-tracker/#race/SEN-KS)).
- **Ballot fights:** A federal judge declined to halt DHS demands that states screen ballots ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/02/us/politics/trump-elections-ballots-states-dhs.html)). The Supreme Court rejected the administration's request to intervene in a California ballot case ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/02/us/politics/trump-supreme-court-california-ballots.html)).
- **Tonight's timing:** The NYT has an hour-by-hour guide to what will be known when ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/03/upshot/poll-closing-times-midterm-elections.html)). NPR explains how it will call races ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/03/g-s1-151311/how-npr-calls-races-2026)).

## Early vote
These are party registrations of voters who cast ballots, not votes.
- **Florida:** 5.46 million ballots, R+2.4, against R+11.4 registration (as of Nov 2, Florida Division of Elections via the [UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/)).
- **North Carolina:** 2.83 million ballots, D 38.4 / R 26.1, against near-even registration (as of Nov 2, [NC State Board data file](https://dl.ncsbe.gov/?prefix=ENRS/2026_11_03/)).
- **Pennsylvania** mail ballots are D+39.5, against D+1.5 registration (as of Nov 2, PA Department of State via the [UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/)). Mail voting skews Democratic.

## Talking points
- Forecasters went into Election Day leaning Democratic on both chambers, and markets agree. Iowa (R+0.3) and South Carolina (R+1.1) have the closest Senate polling averages.
- Courts were active up to the last day: one judge let the DHS ballot-screening demands stand, while the Supreme Court declined the administration's request in the California case.
- Expect results to come in slowly. Starting tomorrow, this brief becomes an election update: calls, every uncalled race, recounts and legal action. Results go up on the site's new Results tab.
