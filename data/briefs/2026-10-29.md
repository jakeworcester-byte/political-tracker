# Midterm brief: Thursday, October 29

## Bottom line
Forecasters rate the House as leaning Democratic; all 10 House forecasters have more seats rated for Democrats, from Inside Elections' 212 D / 205 R to 230 D at The Economist, FiftyPlusOne and Silver Bulletin ([Wikipedia ratings table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_election_ratings)). The Senate is closer: 46 D and 44 R seats are solid or likely, with 10 competitive ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_elections)). Markets price Democratic control at 92.5% for the House and 67.5% for the Senate, each up one point ([Polymarket House](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-house-in-2026), [Senate](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-senate-in-2026)), as of Oct 29.

## Early vote
These are ballots by party registration, not votes, and mail ballots have leaned Democratic since 2020.
- **North Carolina:** Through Oct 27, 1,961,167 ballots were cast, 136% of the 2022 total at the same point (1,440,100) ([NCSBE](https://s3.amazonaws.com/dl.ncsbe.gov/Press/2026%20General/NC%20Absentee%20Stats%20for%202026%20General/2026-10-28%20Daily%20Absentee%20Stats%20Report%20-%202026%20General.pdf)). By Oct 28 the total passed 2.1 million at D 39.8% / R 25.3%; the Democratic edge narrowed 1.1 points in a day ([NCSBE data file](https://dl.ncsbe.gov/?prefix=ENRS/2026_11_03/)).
- **Florida (Oct 28):** 3,896,546 ballots at R 40.2% / D 38.9%, against registration of R 41.5% / D 30.1% ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/), from Florida Division of Elections data).
- **Senate battlegrounds (Oct 28):** Arizona 1,269,928 at D 37.7% / R 38.5% (via a private compiler); Iowa 338,739 at D 48.6% / R 33.1%; Kansas 282,232 at D 44.6% / R 41.2%; Nebraska 185,194 at D 39.7% / R 43.4% ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/), from secretary of state data).
