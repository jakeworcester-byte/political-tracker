# Midterm brief: Friday, October 16

## Bottom line
Forecasters rate the House as leaning Democratic; after Inside Elections' update, all 10 forecasters have more seats rated for Democrats ([Wikipedia ratings table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_election_ratings)). The Senate is closer: 46 D and 44 R seats are solid or likely, with 10 competitive ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_elections)). Markets price Democratic control at 90.5% for the House and 64.5% for the Senate ([Polymarket House](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-house-in-2026), [Senate](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-senate-in-2026)), as of Oct 16.

## What moved
- **Inside Elections (Oct 15):** It now rates the House 212 D / 205 R / 18 Tossup, up from 209 / 210 / 16. All 26 of its House and Senate changes moved toward Democrats. In the House, AZ-01, PA-07 and PA-08 moved to Tilt D, and IA-02, NC-01, NC-11, OH-09 and TX-15 to Tossup ([Wikipedia ratings table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_election_ratings)).
- **Senate (Inside Elections):** Texas moved from Tilt R to Tossup, Kansas from Likely R to Tilt R, and New Hampshire from Tossup to Tilt D ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_elections)).
- **Iowa Senate:** The Economist moved the other way, from Tossup to Lean R.
- **South Carolina Senate:** The consensus moved from Likely R to Lean R, so the race is now tracked. InsiderAdvantage shows Graham +1, ending Sep 29 ([InsiderAdvantage](https://insideradvantage.com/insideradvantage-south-carolina-senate-graham-leads-by-one-point/)).
- **House consensus:** MT-01 moved from Lean R to Tilt R, and AL-02 from Likely R to Lean R.

## Early vote
These are ballots by party registration, not votes, and mail ballots have leaned Democratic since 2020.
- **North Carolina:** In-person early voting opened Oct 15 with 201,609 ballots cast that day, bringing the total from 33,444 to 237,539. The shares are D 46.9% / R 19.5% / other 33.6%, against an even registration split ([NCSBE data file](https://dl.ncsbe.gov/?prefix=ENRS/2026_11_03/)).
- **Florida and Pennsylvania (Oct 15):** Florida has 741,141 mail ballots at D 42.7% / R 36.3%, against registration of D 30.1% / R 41.5% ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/), state data). Pennsylvania has 391,195 at D 69.7% / R 21.9% ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/), state data).
- **Arizona and Iowa (Oct 15):** Arizona has 147,320 at D 41.7% / R 36.2%, via a private compiler; Iowa has 17,384 at D 56.4% / R 28.7% ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/)).
