# Midterm brief: Thursday, October 15

## Bottom line
Forecasters rate the House as leaning Democratic: 9 of 10 House forecasters have more seats rated for Democrats than for Republicans, and Inside Elections is the exception at 209 D / 210 R ([Wikipedia ratings table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_election_ratings)). The Senate is closer: 46 D and 45 R seats are solid or likely, with 9 competitive ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_elections)). Markets price Democratic control at 90.5% for the House ([Polymarket](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-house-in-2026)) and 63.5% for the Senate ([Polymarket](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-senate-in-2026)), as of Oct 15.

## Early vote
Ballots cast by party registration, as of Oct 14 unless noted. Registration is not a vote, and mail ballots have leaned Democratic since 2020.
- **Pennsylvania:** 346,932 mail ballots; D 70.3% / R 21.5% / other 8.2% ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/), from PA Department of State data), against registration that is roughly even.
- **Florida:** 669,634 mail ballots; D 42.8% / R 36.1% / other 21.0% ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/)), against registration of D 30.1% / R 41.5% ([FL Division of Elections, Jul 31](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/data-statistics/voter-registration-statistics/voter-registration-reports/voter-registration-by-party-affiliation/)).
- **North Carolina:** 33,444 ballots; D 44.2% / R 17.0% / other 38.8%, against an even registration split ([NCSBE data file](https://dl.ncsbe.gov/?prefix=ENRS/2026_11_03/)). Through Oct 13, volume was 72% of the 2022 total at the same point ([NCSBE](https://s3.amazonaws.com/dl.ncsbe.gov/Press/2026%20General/NC%20Absentee%20Stats%20for%202026%20General/2026-10-14%20Daily%20Absentee%20Stats%20Report%20-%202026%20General.pdf)).
- **Other Senate battlegrounds:** Maine 59,314 ballots, D 54.8% / R 18.5% (as of Oct 13); Nebraska 62,204, D 45.9% / R 37.0%; Arizona 52,704, D 44.0% / R 33.9% (Arizona figures via a private compiler) ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/)).
