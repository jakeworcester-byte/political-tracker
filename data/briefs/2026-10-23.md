# Midterm brief: Friday, October 23

## Bottom line
Forecasters rate the House as leaning Democratic; all 10 House forecasters have more seats rated for Democrats ([Wikipedia ratings table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_election_ratings)). The Senate is closer: 46 D and 44 R seats are solid or likely, with 10 competitive ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_elections)). Markets price Democratic control at 92.5% for the House and 67.5% for the Senate, the Senate up one point ([Polymarket House](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-house-in-2026), [Senate](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-senate-in-2026)), as of Oct 23.

## Early vote
These are ballots by party registration, not votes, and mail ballots have leaned Democratic since 2020.
- **North Carolina:** Through Oct 21, 1,101,321 ballots were cast, 138% of the 2022 total at the same point (798,778) ([NCSBE](https://s3.amazonaws.com/dl.ncsbe.gov/Press/2026%20General/NC%20Absentee%20Stats%20for%202026%20General/2026-10-22%20Daily%20Absentee%20Stats%20Report%20-%202026%20General.pdf)). By Oct 22 the total reached 1,269,919 at D 42.2% / R 23.3% / other 34.4%, with the Democratic edge narrowing as in-person voting grows ([NCSBE data file](https://dl.ncsbe.gov/?prefix=ENRS/2026_11_03/)).
- **Large states (Oct 22):** Florida has 1,981,475 ballots (763,666 in person) at D 40.6% / R 38.9%, against registration of D 30.1% / R 41.5%; California 2,478,771 at D 49.9% / R 29.0%; Pennsylvania 723,055 at D 67.0% / R 24.1% ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/), from secretary of state data).
- **Senate battlegrounds (Oct 22):** Arizona 743,697 at D 38.8% / R 38.4% (via a private compiler); Iowa 167,195 at D 51.7% / R 31.4%; Nebraska 125,954 at D 41.9% / R 41.4% ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/)).
