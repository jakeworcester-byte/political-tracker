# Midterm brief: Tuesday, October 20

## Bottom line
Forecasters rate the House as leaning Democratic; all 10 House forecasters have more seats rated for Democrats, and The Economist now has 230 D / 190 R ([Wikipedia ratings table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_election_ratings)). The Senate is closer: 47 D and 45 R seats are solid or likely, with 8 competitive ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_elections)). Markets price Democratic control at 91.5% for the House and 64.5% for the Senate ([Polymarket House](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-house-in-2026), [Senate](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-senate-in-2026)), as of Oct 20.

## Early vote
These are ballots by party registration, not votes, and mail ballots have leaned Democratic since 2020.
- **North Carolina:** Through Oct 18, 525,970 ballots were cast, 137% of the 2022 total at the same point (383,029) ([NCSBE](https://s3.amazonaws.com/dl.ncsbe.gov/Press/2026%20General/NC%20Absentee%20Stats%20for%202026%20General/2026-10-19%20Daily%20Absentee%20Stats%20Report%20-%202026%20General.pdf)). By Oct 19 the total reached 733,297, at D 44.7% / R 20.7% / other 34.6% against an even registration split; the Democratic edge narrowed 2.1 points in a day ([NCSBE data file](https://dl.ncsbe.gov/?prefix=ENRS/2026_11_03/)).
- **Large states (Oct 19):** Florida reached 1,102,430 ballots, including 171,025 in person, at D 41.9% / R 37.1% against registration of D 30.1% / R 41.5%. California has 1,548,252 at D 48.5% / R 29.3%; Pennsylvania 532,067 at D 68.3% / R 23.1% ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/), from secretary of state data).
- **Senate battlegrounds (Oct 19):** Arizona has 388,170 at D 39.7% / R 38.0% (via a private compiler); Iowa 61,021 at D 53.8% / R 28.9%; Nebraska 90,039 at D 44.1% / R 39.0% ([UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/)).
