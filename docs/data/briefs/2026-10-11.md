# Midterm brief: Sunday, October 11

## Bottom line

Twenty-three days out, forecasters and markets agree on the shape of the race but not the result. The House leans Democratic: nine of the ten forecasters compiled on the Wikipedia ratings table project more Democratic than Republican seats before tossups, with Inside Elections the exception at 210 R to 209 D, and Polymarket prices a Democratic House majority at 90.5% ([ratings table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_election_ratings), [market](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-house-in-2026)). The Senate is the real contest: by consensus rating Democrats sit at 46 seats solid or likely, Republicans at 45, with nine races in the Lean-to-Tossup band deciding a 51-seat majority ([Senate ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_elections)). Polymarket prices Democratic Senate control at 63.5% ([market](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-senate-in-2026)).

## Talking points

- The generic ballot averages D+7.2 across four recent polls, and the president's net approval averages minus 25.5, the kind of environment that historically costs the president's party seats ([polls via VoteHub](https://votehub.com)).
- The Senate majority runs through six consensus Tossups, all currently Republican-held: Alaska, Iowa (open), Kansas, Maine, Texas, and the Ohio special. Democrats need a net gain of four seats.
- Affordability is the shared vocabulary: a WSJ analysis found right-leaning podcasters use the term about as often as left-leaning ones ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/politics/elections/conservative-podcasters-cant-stop-talking-about-affordability-ab863294)).
